Turbulence · Computation · Discovery

Understanding flows across scales

Research

Our research brings together fluid mechanics, applied mathematics and scientific computing. We seek to understand the physical mechanisms of complex flows and to develop computational methods for their prediction.

Turbulence theory & direct numerical simulation

Turbulent motion couples a wide range of spatial and temporal scales. We study its statistical properties, coherent structures and energy transfer, including the inverse energy cascade in two-dimensional turbulence and the effects of compressibility in three-dimensional flows.

Lattice Boltzmann methods

Mesoscopic descriptions offer an alternative route to modelling fluid motion. Our work on lattice Boltzmann methods connects kinetic models to macroscopic hydrodynamics, with applications to multiphase flow, complex boundaries and transport in porous media.

Large-eddy simulation & flow modelling

Resolving every scale in an engineering flow can be prohibitively expensive. Large-eddy simulation and subgrid-scale models address this challenge. Constrained modelling uses physical and statistical information to improve predictions of wall-bounded and compressible turbulent flows.

Multiscale computation & applications

We study methods that bridge continuum and molecular descriptions, as well as multiphase flow, combustion-related physics and industrial applications of turbulence. Scientific computing and industrial software connect these methods to practical problems.
